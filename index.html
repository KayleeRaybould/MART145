Understanding the potential health risks of a widely available herbal product

What Is Kratom?

Kratom is a tropical tree called Mitragyna speciosa that is native to Southeast Asia. Its leaves contain compounds that can affect receptors in the brain, including receptors involved in opioid effects. Kratom is sold in different forms, including dried leaves, powders, capsules, extracts, and other products.

Why Can Kratom Be Dangerous?

Kratom is sometimes described as a natural or safer alternative to other substances, but natural does not automatically mean safe. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) warns consumers not to use kratom because of serious potential risks, including liver toxicity, seizures, and substance use disorder.

Kratom can also produce opioid-like effects. Depending on the product and circumstances, reported effects can include nausea, vomiting, constipation, sedation, physical dependence, withdrawal, and respiratory depression. In rare cases, deaths have been associated with kratom use, although the FDA notes that other drugs were often involved and the contribution of kratom is not always clear.

Major Health Risks

One concern is liver toxicity. Some people who use kratom have developed liver problems. Another concern is seizures, which have been reported after kratom use. Kratom can also lead to dependence and withdrawal. A person may develop tolerance, cravings, or withdrawal symptoms after stopping regular use.

Product quality is another concern. The FDA has warned about some kratom products being contaminated with Salmonella or containing concerning levels of heavy metals. Because products can vary in their ingredients and strength, it can be difficult for consumers to know exactly what they are getting.

Dependence and Withdrawal

Regular kratom use can result in physical dependence for some people. Withdrawal can happen when a person who has become dependent stops using it. The FDA has described symptoms and patterns such as cravings, tolerance, continued use despite negative consequences, and withdrawal. This is important because a product being sold as an herbal product does not mean that it cannot have addictive or dependence-related effects.

What Should People Remember?

The biggest lesson is that kratom should not automatically be considered harmless just because it comes from a plant. Scientific knowledge about kratom is still developing, and products can vary widely. Anyone who is concerned about kratom use or is experiencing a reaction should talk with a healthcare professional. For an emergency or suspected poisoning, contact emergency services or Poison Help.
